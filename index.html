The free prompt pack (DIY route)

If you want to do it yourself with ChatGPT Plus ($20/mo) — and only for non-client-facing work, never with privileged financials — here's the 5-prompt system we use to draft client emails, advisory commentary, and tax-research questions. Save these in a Notion doc.

  1. Client email from notes — "Convert these rough notes into a 200-word client email: [paste notes]. Tone: warm but professional, no jargon. End with one clear ask (decision or document needed) and a date by which."
  2. Variance commentary — "Compare these two P&Ls (current month vs prior month): [paste P&L]. Identify the 5 largest variance lines. For each, write a one-sentence explanation a non-accountant client would understand. Flag any that need follow-up questions."
  3. Tax-research question — "I'm a CPA researching [specific issue]. List the 5 sub-questions a tax attorney would ask to scope this properly. For each sub-question, name the relevant IRC section, Treasury Regulation, and most recent significant case. Flag any that are unsettled."
  4. Advisory recommendations — "Client profile: [paste summary]. Generate 3 specific advisory recommendations for the next 90 days. Each should be actionable, measurable, and tied to a financial outcome (cash, tax savings, or risk reduction). Avoid generic 'consider reviewing' language."
  5. Engagement letter scope — "Draft an engagement letter scope for [service type] for a client with [profile]. Include deliverables, fee structure, timeline, and explicit out-of-scope items. Match the tone of the AICPA sample engagement letter but in plain English."

The total time per task: 10-20 minutes for a first pass. Quality: useful for non-privileged work only — never for documents that contain client tax returns or financial statements unless your firm has signed an enterprise agreement with the AI provider.

Questions accountants actually ask

What is the single best AI tool for an accountant just starting out?
A bookkeeping automation tool like Bench (outsourced) or Botkeeper (in-house), plus a tax-research tool like TaxGPT. One replaces 20-40 hours/month of categorization; the other cuts research time by half. Together they're $300-$500/mo depending on client volume.
Are these AI tools safe for confidential client financial data?
The serious ones (Bench, Botkeeper, TaxGPT, Karbon) have SOC 2 Type II, explicit confidentiality terms, and contractual data-handling agreements that satisfy IRS Circular 230 obligations. Generic consumer AI (ChatGPT free/Plus, Claude.ai, Gemini free) does not — those services retain user inputs and may use them for training. Never paste client financials, tax returns, or bank statements into consumer AI tools unless you have a signed enterprise agreement.
Do these tools replace junior accountants?
No — and any vendor that tells you they do is overselling. They replace the categorization, reconciliation, and first-pass review work. The judgment work (advisory, complex tax positions, audit conclusions, client relationships) still needs a human CPA. What changes is leverage: one accountant with AI tools serves 2-3× the clients they could before.
Will AI put accountants out of work?
It will put accountants who refuse to use AI at a 30-50% efficiency disadvantage against accountants who do. The compounding math is stark: an AI-equipped accountant serves more clients, bills more hours, and earns more advisory revenue. The accountant who doesn't gets slowly outcompeted on both price and capacity.
What's the worst AI tool you've reviewed?
Anything that costs more than $500/mo and calls itself an "AI-powered accounting platform" but is really a QBO/Xero clone with a ChatGPT plugin stapled on. If the demo is "ask our AI anything about your books" and the answer is generic, walk away. The bar to be useful in accounting AI is high because the data sensitivity is real and the work is structured — most startups don't clear it.
